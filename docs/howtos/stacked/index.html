Stacked Underground Combo — the Other Meter Type

This is the other meter type: the stacked Siemens combo — meter section on top, breaker section below — fed underground, on the same TUG job. Crews will meet this can on other houses, so it gets its own lesson. The can is different; the TUG underneath is the same four moves: combo on the wall · breaker and GFI in, and marked · grounding to the UFER in PVC pipe · grounding bridge on — then covers, labels, and photos. The drawings on this page come from sheet E-7 of Sharma's typical-details set.

Safety first: you are setting gear the utility will energize. Work it dead, keep hands out of the meter socket, and never cut a utility seal or pull a meter unless the utility and your foreman say so.
Grounding at Sharma — UFER first: typically we ground the TUG to the UFER (the concrete-encased electrode in the footing), with the grounding conductor run in PVC pipe. Find the stub before you start — the UFER section below is taught before the steps for a reason.

UNDERGROUND SIDE-BY-SIDE — THE SHARMA TUG OVERHEAD INSTRUCTIONS

Before you roll

SHARMA STANDARD
  • Gear: this lesson's can is the stacked Siemens meter main combo — meter section on top, breaker section below (200A main). On the truck for a TUG: the combo, a 20A breaker, a GFCI receptacle with weatherproof box and a listed extra-duty while-in-use (bubble) cover — the deep cover that stays weatherproof with a cord plugged in, not a regular flip cover, ½-inch PVC pipe for the grounding run (the copper runs in ½-in PVC), PVC male adapters, locknuts, and bushings for every pipe entry, the intersystem grounding bridge, grounding conductor and listed clamps, a 90° sweep and 2-inch Schedule 40 PVC for the underground downpipe, pipe straps — 2 minimum for the downpipe (Mineralac best, 2-hole rigid straps work too), labels.
  • Location: where the plan and the utility say — on Sharma houses the meter goes on the garage side. Meter height is the utility's rule — check it, don't guess.
  • Find the UFER stub first. Know where the grounding lands before the combo goes up — it decides where your PVC run goes.

The UFER — concrete-encased electrode

NEC 250.52(A)(3)NEC 250.66(B)TYPICALLY, THIS IS THE GROUND

Named for Herbert Ufer. A UFER is a grounding electrode buried in the concrete footing itself: at least 20 ft of either ½-inch rebar (one piece, or pieces tied together with steel tie wire) or bare copper conductor — #4 AWG is the size typically run in the areas we work — encased in at least 2 inches of concrete, near the bottom of the footing, in direct contact with the earth. Concrete holds moisture against the steel/copper, so a UFER is one of the best electrodes there is — better than a driven rod.

Working the UFER on a Sharma job

  • Protect the stub. The rebar or copper tail sticking up is the whole connection point. Don't let it get cut off, bent flat, or buried. If a stub is cut or missing, tell the foreman — don't just drive a rod and stay quiet.
  • Run it in PVC. Sharma runs the grounding conductor from the combo down to the UFER protected in PVC pipe — that's the pipe you see down the wall in the front-view drawing (sheet E-7.2).
  • Connect it right: listed clamp/connector made for rebar/concrete-encased use. #4 copper is the largest GEC the code requires to a UFER (250.66(B)) — bigger is allowed, never required.
  • Every electrode present bonds in (250.50). If a job also has rods or a metal water pipe, they all tie into the one system. And a UFER is used when the plan/footing actually has one — never claim one that isn't there.

No UFER on the job? The ground rod rule

  • Two rods. Where no UFER is present, the ground is two 8-ft ground rods, spaced at least 6 ft apart, driven so the full 8 ft is in contact with the soil (250.53(A)). Sharma standard: ⅝-in × 8-ft rods with acorn clamps.
  • GEC to rods: #6 copper is the largest the code ever requires (250.66(A)), whatever the service size. Sharma's default GEC is #4 bare copper.
  • One system. Rods bond together with every other electrode present (250.50) — never leave a rod floating on its own.

The TUG process — four moves

MOVE 1 · Meter combo on the wall — garage side

Mount the meter main combo at the planned location (garage side), at the utility's meter height, plumb and solid into the structure — not just the finish. A crooked can is the first thing the inspector and the builder see. Every pipe that enters the can lands on a male adapter, with the locknut run up tight inside and a bushing over the threads where the conductors pass — very important, and it's its own line on the Sharma TUG checklist. One exception: the small ground hole in the bottom of the can, where the grounding conductor leaves — that one typically doesn't need a bushing. Great practice when the can allows it: if the combo has a ½-inch knockout toward the very back of the can, out of the way of the underground pipe, bring the ground out there with a bushing. But that's not typical at all — most combos don't lay out that way, so the small ground hole is the norm.

The underground riser (downpipe) enters the bottom of the can: 2-inch Schedule 40 PVC, down to 3 feet under grade, a 90° sweep attached at the bottom with the sweep pointed toward the street. Strap the downpipe to the wall — 2 straps minimum. Mineralac straps are best — for both overhead and underground services; 2-hole rigid straps work too. This is a power company requirement (Duke Energy and SECO), not a Sharma preference — build it their way or the service doesn't get connected.

SHARMA TUG CHECKLIST — ITEM 1 + 4

Very important — male adapter · locknut · bushing. No pipe stubbed into the can raw. Male adapter on the pipe, locknut tight inside the can, bushing on the threads so nothing sharp touches the conductors. The only place you skip the bushing is the small ground hole — unless the can has that ½-inch knockout toward the very back, clear of the underground pipe; then use it, with a bushing. Great practice, but not typical.

2 · Install the breaker and the GFI — and mark it

MOVE 2 · 20A breaker + TUG Service GFCI outlet — installed and MARKED

Set a 20A breaker in the combo and feed the TUG Service GFCI Outlet in its weatherproof box under the breaker section, fitted with a listed extra-duty while-in-use cover — the deep bubble cover that closes over a plugged-in cord and stays weatherproof while it's in use (NEC 406.9(B)). Not a regular flip cover: a flip lid only protects the outlet when nothing is plugged in, and the TUG outlet lives with a cord in it. Then mark it: the breaker labeled in the combo, and the GFI marked as the TUG service outlet. That one receptacle is the power the whole build runs on until the house is finished — it has to be right, protected, and findable.

3 · Grounding to the UFER, in PVC pipe

MOVE 3 · Grounding to the UFER — copper in PVC

Land the grounding conductor on the combo's ground/neutral bar, take it out through the small ground hole in the bottom of the can (that hole typically needs no bushing — or, if this can has a ½-inch knockout toward the very back, clear of the underground pipe, use that with a bushing; great practice, not typical), then run it down the wall in ½-inch PVC pipe for protection and connect it to the UFER stub with a listed clamp. This is typically the whole grounding job on a Sharma TUG — the UFER is the electrode (section above). If the plan shows rods as well, drive and bond them too; every electrode present ties together. No UFER present? Then the ground rod rule below applies: two 8-ft rods, at least 6 ft apart.

4 · Install the grounding bridge

MOVE 4 · Intersystem grounding bridge

Mount the intersystem grounding bridge in-line on the grounding (GEC) PVC pipe run below the combo — the pipe passes in the top of it and out the bottom, the body sits flat on the wall — and bond it to the grounding system. The copper runs in ½-inch PVC, and at the bridge the PVC is cut in two — one piece above the bridge, one piece below — landing straight into the bridge with no fittings. (The only time we don't use fittings on PVC.) Strap the run with typically 2 straps: one between the bridge and the meter, one between the bridge and the floor grade — each at the halfway mark of its PVC piece. The bridge is the landing point where cable, phone, and fiber grounds will bond to Sharma's ground — it goes on at the TUG, not as an afterthought, and it gets its own close-up photo (below).

NEC 250.94 — intersystem bondingSHARMA TUG CHECKLIST — ITEM 3

The bridge run, drawn simply — step by step

  1. Copper comes out of the combo and runs down inside ½-inch PVC — that's piece 1.
  2. Strap 1 goes at the halfway mark between the meter and the bridge.
  3. The PVC is cut in two and the grounding bridge goes in-line — pipe in the top, out the bottom, no fittings.
  4. Piece 2 of the PVC continues below the bridge toward the floor.
  5. Strap 2 goes at the halfway mark between the bridge and the floor grade — then the copper carries on to the UFER.

5 · Close it up, mark it, photograph it

  • EMERGENCY DISCONNECT label on the combo door — the main breaker is the service disconnect (230.85).
  • Covers on, the GFI's extra-duty in-use cover closed over the outlet, everything tight and torqued to the door-label spec.
  • Photos for the job record: the grounding bridge + GFI close-up, and the TUG complete — wide shot of the whole set-up. No photos, it didn't happen.

Common TUG fails — don't be these

  • GFI in but not marked. An unmarked breaker and outlet is a fail on Sharma's list — mark it, every time.
  • Regular flip cover on the TUG GFI. Wrong cover — a flip lid leaves a plugged-in cord exposed to the weather. The TUG outlet takes a listed extra-duty while-in-use (bubble) cover, per 406.9(B).
  • Grounding left bare down the wall. The grounding run to the UFER goes in PVC pipe for protection — that's the Sharma way and it's in the drawings.
  • No grounding bridge. The intersystem bridge is part of the TUG, not a later trip. Forgotten bridge = return trip.
  • Loose locknuts, missing bushings, pipe stubbed in with no male adapter. It's a named checklist item because it gets missed. Check every conduit entry — then leave the small ground hole alone, it doesn't take one, typically.
  • Downpipe wrong, too shallow, sweep pointed the wrong way, or short on straps. The downpipe is 2-inch Schedule 40 PVC. Three feet under, 90° sweep on the bottom pointed toward the street, 2 straps minimum — it's the power company's requirement (Duke and SECO), every time.
  • Cut or buried UFER stub, nobody told. If the stub is damaged or missing, the foreman hears it from you that day.
  • No wide shot. The TUG isn't complete on paper until the complete wide shot is on the job record.
